# Release 2026-10-26.7 — the weather slide and the array's size

The third slide. **Weather** shows the conditions right now, a seven-day
solar forecast for your own roof, and the week ahead, laid out for the
7" 800×480 screen.

![the weather slide at 800x480](../preview-weather-slide.png)

## The place

**Settings → Weather & solar forecast**. Type a town, press **Find**, and
the latitude and longitude are filled in for you.

The gazetteer Open-Meteo uses only knows towns and villages, so a hamlet
or a street will not be found — use the town nearby. Gosden, for one, is
not in it; Welwyn Garden City is.

The rest of the settings are the units (Celsius or Fahrenheit, and km/h,
m/s, mph or knots), which way the panels face, and the roof's tilt.

## The array's size, from your own yield

This is the part worth having.

The forecast knows how much sun lands on a roof. It does not know how much
of that *your* array collects — that depends on the panels, the angle, the
dirt, the heat and the inverter. So the dashboard measures it:

```
system factor = the kWh your inverter made ÷ the kWh per m² of sun that fell
```

The inverter's daily PV yield comes from the FoxESS production report —
the same figure the Fox app shows — and the sun comes from the forecast's
*global tilted irradiance* for those same days. The two are compared day
for day, and the **median** of the daily ratios is the factor. The median,
so one odd day does not throw the whole thing out.

That factor is what turns tomorrow's forecast into tomorrow's yield:

```
tomorrow's kWh = tomorrow's kWh per m² of sun × the system factor
```

And the size of the array follows from it:

```
kWp = the system factor ÷ the performance ratio
```

The performance ratio (0.85 by default) is an **assumption** — the share
of the sun an array collects once the inverter, the cabling, the heat and
the dirt have taken their cut. So the kWp is an **estimate**, and the
system factor is a **measurement**. The panel says which is which:

```
array ≈ 4.9 kWp · fitted from 27 days · 14.2 kWh tomorrow
```

The window reaches back past the turn of the month, so the fit does not
reset on the first of every month. The month before is fetched once and
kept.

## The roof

The forecast's sun is for a plane at a given tilt and azimuth, so both are
settings. **Azimuth 0 is south** and Open-Meteo counts anticlockwise from
there, so +90 is west and −90 is east. About 35° collects the most in
Britain.

## Before it has been fitted

Until there are enough days to compare, the panel shows the sun in kWh/m²
rather than a yield it cannot stand behind, and says so.

## Also

- The icons are drawn inline — no files to fetch, and they follow the
  theme.
- The Detected table on the settings page shows the place, the array's
  size, the factor, the days that went into the fit, and how today's
  half-day is comparing with it.
- **Test the forecast** tries the settings and says what came back.
- The forecast is polled every fifteen minutes, and the worker never
  dies — a failure is shown on the slide and retried.
- On the smallest screens the details are dropped rather than the type
  shrunk: the list of details goes, the wind rides along with the
  conditions, and the day cards lose their wind and rain lines.

## A bug found on the way

Turning the rotation off in the settings used to **move the slide** — the
rotator was asleep for the length of the interval, so it only found out
about the change when it woke up, and it advanced the slide on the way
out. It now notices the change within half a second and stops where it
is.

## The proof

The factor is checked against a known answer: with a pretend array that
collects exactly 4.2 kWh for every kWh/m², the fit recovers 4.2 and
4.94 kWp.

48 layout combinations pass — two diagrams, three slides, eight viewport
sizes — and 300 tests (was 247).
