# Release 2026-11-01.1 — a bigger inverter

The rings diagram puts the inverter in the middle — and the middle was the
smallest thing on it. The hub's ring was 68 units against the satellites'
50, which is not much of a difference for the thing the whole diagram is
about. It is 78 now.

## What changed

The hub — the ring with the state of charge in it — grew from a radius of
68 to a radius of 78, and its name and its firmware moved down with it.

That is the whole of it, and it is four kinds of thing:

- the **ring itself**: the track, the arc and the circle behind it, all
  from 68 to 78;
- the **four wires**, which end on the ring's edge and so have to be
  measured out to it again;
- the **two labels** under the hub, which sit below the ring and so have
  to move down by the same amount the ring moved out;
- the **pictures**, which were photographs of the old design.

## The wires

A wire that ends short of the ring leaves a gap, and a wire that ends
short of it by a different amount on each side is worse. So the four
wires were re-measured: each end is now on the new ring, to within a
hundredth of a unit.

The control points moved with the ends, by the same vector. That is what
keeps the wire looking the same: the bow stays the same, and the wire
meets the ring at the same angle it did before, because the tangent at the
end of a quadratic Bézier is the same when both of its last two points
move together.

```
distance from the hub's centre to the end of each wire:
  before: 68.00  68.00  68.00  68.00
  after:  78.00  78.00  78.00  78.00
```

## The labels

The `INVERTER` label sits below the ring, and it cleared it by 2.5 units —
about two and a half pixels on the Pi. Growing the ring by ten units
without moving the label would have drawn the word straight across the
ring.

So the label and the firmware line moved down by the same ten units, and
the gap between them and the ring is what it was: **2.1 to 2.7 units**,
depending on the screen. There is still 19 units of drawing below the
firmware line, so the diagram still fits its own box.

## What it looks like

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the hub's radius | 68 | **78** |
| the satellites' radius | 50 | 50 |
| the hub on the Pi (800×480) | 48 px | **56 px** |
| the hub on a desktop (1440×900) | 106 px | **121 px** |
| the gap between the ring and the name | 2.5 units | 2.5 units |

## How it was checked that nothing else moved

The geometry of every panel, tile, table row and SVG label was measured
before and after, at nine sizes on three slides, and compared element by
element — 102 elements a case, not a summary of them.

**Only two elements moved**: the `INVERTER` label and the firmware line
under it, in the four cases that show the rings diagram. Every panel,
every tile, every table row and every other piece of type on the diagram
is identical, and the other twenty-three cases are identical outright.

The **layout sweep** passes: ten viewport sizes, three slides, two diagram
styles, sixty cases. That includes the check that the text inside each
ring fits inside the circle — the hub's `100%` has a chord of 153 units
to sit in and needs 64.

The **diagram's own tests** were already written for this: the wires are
checked against the rings they are supposed to join, and the rings are
read out of the markup rather than written out in the test — so the hub
could be made bigger without the test going stale. It caught nothing this
time, which is what it is for.

## Also

- Four new tests for the hub itself: that it is as big as it was made,
  that its name clears the ring rather than crossing it, that the
  firmware sits below the name, and that the whole thing still fits
  inside the drawing.
- The pictures of the rings diagram — the README's front page, the
  low-battery one and the gallery in `docs/` — were re-taken, because
  they were photographs of the old ring. The low-battery one is now
  consistent: the diagram and the Live panel both say 9 %, where before
  the diagram said 9 % and the panel said 82 %.
