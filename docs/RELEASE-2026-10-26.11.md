# Release 2026-10-26.11 — the slide that is dealt onto the deck

The slide transition blanked the old slide before the new one came in.
Now the new slide is dealt onto the top of the old one, the way a card
goes onto a deck, and the old one dissolves underneath it.

The header also carries the date now, not just the time.

## The transition

The slide used to change by swapping the contents of one element: the
new slide's panels were shown, the old slide's panels were hidden, and
the panels that were on screen a moment ago were simply gone. It was a
cut, not a move.

The new slide comes in from the right, over the top of the one that is
leaving. To do that there have to be two slides on screen at once, so
the one that is leaving is **copied** and the copy is left in the stage
behind the live slide:

```html
<div class="stage">
  <div class="dash" id="dash">...</div>   <!-- the slide that is showing -->
  <div class="dash ghost">...</div>       <!-- the one that is leaving -->
</div>
```

The copy is made **before** the new slide is put in place, so it still
holds the old slide. It is put in the stage *after* the live slide
rather than before it, because the copy has the same element ids in it
as the live slide, and the live one has to be the one `getElementById`
finds.

Then:

- the copy fades out over 560 ms, under the slide arriving;
- the new slide slides in from the right over 420 ms, throwing a shadow
  onto the copy as it goes, so it reads as a card rather than as a
  picture sliding past;
- the copy is taken away when it has finished fading.

The header does not move: it sits outside the stage, and its box is
the same before, during and after the change.

## The slide owns its grid

The grid for each slide used to be written against the body:

```css
body[data-slide="power"] .dash{...}
```

That is a problem now. The body can only say one slide at a time, so
the copy of the leaving slide would have been told to show the *new*
slide's grid, and the two slides would have fought over the same rules.
The rules are written against the slide itself now:

```css
body .dash[data-slide="power"]{...}
```

The body is still in the selector, and it is there for a reason: the
layout setting (`full`, `compact`, `tiny`) is written against the body
as well, and it must not win. Before, the slide rules were the later
of the two, so the slide owned the grid whatever the layout was set to.
The `body` in the selector puts the slide rules back on an equal
footing, and the slide still wins on the order they appear in the file.

## The date in the header

The top of the screen is the one part of the dashboard that does not
change with the slide, so it is the right place to say what day it is:

```
Your plant  1B2C3D4E5F60 · WLAN-WW (WiFi & LAN)   [LIVE] [0.2s] [Tue 27 Oct 2026 · 09:44] [⚙]
```

The parts are asked for separately, because en-GB puts a comma between
the weekday and the date and there is no room for it.

The header is one line, so something has to give when the screen is
small. The weekday goes first, and on the smallest screens the plant
details go before the plant name:

| screen | the header says |
|---|---|
| 1280x720 and up | `Tue 27 Oct 2026 · 09:44` |
| 800x480 (the Pi) | `27 Oct 2026 · 09:44` |
| 320x240 | `27 Oct 2026 · 09:44`, and the plant name only |

## Testing

- The transition is driven from the page rather than from the
  stylesheet, because a CSS animation runs once and then keeps its
  final state: the rule for a slide stays matched while that slide is
  showing, so the animation would not run again on the next change.
  Someone who has asked for less movement gets none.
- Measured in the browser: the outgoing copy is on screen at the start
  and gone by the end, it fades on the way, and the header's box is
  identical before and after.
- The grid was measured for every slide against every layout setting
  and is the same as it was before the change.

## Two tests that had gone stale

The date the work was done on, the 27th, was the first day those two
tests failed on — and neither had anything to do with the calendar
being wrong. They were pinned to dates that have since gone by.

- The **daily solar** test built a history from the 5th to the 7th of
  October and then asked for the days from it. The history only keeps
  three weeks, so the days it asked for had fallen out of the window.
  The window it asks for is now wide enough to reach the history it
  made.
- The **Octopus Mini** test used readings from the 26th to work out
  "today's" totals. "Today" means the local day, so the readings have
  to be from today — they are now worked out from today's midnight.

342 tests (was 332).
