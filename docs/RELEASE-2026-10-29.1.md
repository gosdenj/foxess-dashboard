# Release 2026-10-29.1 — the dashboard on a phone

The dashboard was laid out for a Raspberry Pi's 7" panel (800×480), an ESP32
(320×240) and a desktop. A phone fell through none of those breakpoints and
landed at the smallest one, so it got the leftovers: the diagram dropped,
today's figures dropped, the Octopus figures dropped, the solar forecast
dropped, and 13 px text. It was the Pi with the interesting parts taken out.

A phone now has a layout of its own.

## What it looks like

The phone is in portrait, the numbers are the hero, and the slide is allowed to
scroll.

| Slide | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Power** | the battery ring beside the four live figures, today's figures as a table underneath |
| **Usage** | the chart, the Octopus figures and today's tiles |
| **Weather** | the conditions, the solar forecast and the week |

The **diagram is gone**, and that is deliberate rather than a compromise: it is
a 900-unit-wide drawing, so at 390 px its own type comes out at 11 px. No
amount of CSS makes that legible — the drawing has to be made for the width,
which is a different piece of work.

**The swipe still turns the deck.** The browser is given the vertical pan and
keeps its hands off the horizontal one, so a sideways drag is still the
dashboard's. A phone shows the same slide as the Pi at the same moment,
because the server owns the slide.

## The rule that keeps it off everything else

```css
@media (max-width:560px) and (min-height:600px){ ... }
```

Both halves matter. The width excludes the Pi, the small LCDs, the ESP32 and
the narrow 320×480 panel; the height excludes anything wide but short. **No
display that exists today can match it**, and `tests/test_phone_layout.py`
says so.

## Three things that had to be got right

Each of them was got wrong first.

1. **The swipe.** `touch-action:pan-y` gives the browser the vertical drag and
   leaves the horizontal one, so a sideways swipe is still the deck's. The
   gesture code itself was not touched: the same 60 px, the same sideways
   slop, the same `pointercancel` that forgets a gesture the browser has taken
   for a scroll.
2. **The panels.** A grid row cannot size itself to a panel's content when
   the panel is allowed to shrink, and a panel that shrinks clips its own last
   row — the "Today" panel was losing its last line by two pixels at 360×800.
   The phone's slide is therefore a **column of panels**, each as tall as it
   needs to be, rather than a grid.
3. **The tiles.** The usage slide puts today's tiles in six columns on a
   narrow screen, and six columns of "9,999.99 kWh" is a number with its end
   cut off. On a phone they are two columns.

The chart legend was also cut off at 390 px — one flex row with no wrap. On a
phone it wraps; the smaller screens are left exactly as they were, because at
320 px there is no room for a second row either.

## How it was checked that nothing else moved

`layout_check.py` now knows about the phone and enforces **higher** floors for
it than for the fixed panels (body 14 px, figures 20 px), because a phone is
read closer than a panel on a wall.

On top of that, the geometry of every existing display was measured before and
after the change — the position, size and type size of every panel, tile,
table row and SVG label, at seven sizes on three slides — and compared:

```
geometry identical: 21/27
  (the six that changed are the two phone sizes on the three slides)
```

**Not one pixel of the Pi, the desktop, the laptop, the tablet, the small LCD,
the ESP32 or the narrow panel moved.** The only change the six phone cases
report is `touch-action`, from `none` to `pan-y`.

## Also

- **A test of the calendar, rather than of the sun.** The solar calibration
  compares the inverter's yield against three weeks of forecast, and that
  window moves with the calendar. The weather tests used a fixed set of
  October dates, so on 29 October they fell out of the window and the
  fitting test failed on its own. The fixtures are counted back from today
  now, so they cannot expire — and the assertions about how a day is split
  at midnight are made in a way that holds whether the plant is on summer
  time or not.
- **The real plant name was in the repository.** The ESP32 render test used it
  as a fixture, and the test writes a PNG — so `docs/esp32-day.png` and
  `docs/esp32-evening.png` had the plant's name drawn on them, in a public
  repository. The fixture now uses a generic name and the two images have
  been re-rendered. The inverter serial was never committed.
- The mock-ups that led to this are kept as the record of what the other two
  options would have looked like — a landscape layout, and a portrait layout
  that keeps the diagram. The gallery is at
  [`docs/mockups/index.html`](mockups/index.html), and the investigation is
  in [`docs/MOBILE.md`](MOBILE.md).
- The mock-up stylesheet is a **proposal layer**, kept out of the shipped
  page: `mockups/mobile.css` is never served, and it is what the alternatives
  would be built from if they were ever wanted.
- The renderer can now photograph the **shipped** page as its own candidate,
  so the gallery leads with the real thing rather than with a proposal.
  The photographs of the old layout are kept as the record of what a phone
  used to get; they cannot be re-rendered, because the code that drew them
  has changed.
- The README gained an **On a phone** section, a phone row in the display
  targets, and the file tree now lists the new test.

## Testing

- 485 tests (was 471): fourteen for the phone layout. The only failure is the
  pre-existing one — `test_esp32_render` needs Pillow, which is not installed.
- The full layout sweep passes: ten viewport sizes, three slides, two diagram
  styles.
- The geometry of every existing display was measured before and after:
  **21 of 27 identical**, and the six that changed are the two phone sizes on
  the three slides, in `touch-action` alone (`none` → `pan-y`).
- The swipe was verified on a real phone viewport: a 220 px horizontal drag
  turned the deck from the power slide to the usage slide.
