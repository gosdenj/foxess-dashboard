# Release 2026-10-27.2 — the daylight band

The weather slide grows a picture of the day: a strip of the whole day,
from midnight to midnight, with the twilight either side of the sun and
a marker showing where the sun is now.

## What it looks like

The RIGHT NOW panel used to end with a row that said:

```
Sun                                              07:12 – 17:54
```

It now says the same thing, and says it with a picture:

```
DAYLIGHT                    9h 55m · 5h 22m of daylight left
▓▓▓▓▓░░▒▒▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░▒▒░░▓▓▓▓▓
     06:47 sunrise                    16:42 sunset
```

The strip is the whole day. The colours are the light: black for the
night, then the three twilights — astronomical, nautical and civil — and
then the amber of the day. The bead on the strip is the sun's own
place, so the strip doubles as a clock.

The band takes the place of the old `Sun` row, so nothing is lost and
nothing had to be squeezed in — the weather slide is already full at
800×480, and every one of the other panels is untouched.

## Where the twilight comes from

The forecast gives sunrise and sunset, but not the twilight around
them, so that is worked out locally, in `foxess/sun.py`. It is the
standard solar position formula — the same one the reference sunrise
sites use — and it needs nothing outside the standard library.

The times it works out were checked against the forecast service's own
sunrise and sunset for ten places and days, from Welwyn Garden City to
Sydney to Reykjavík: the worst difference is under three minutes, and
usually under one. The forecast's times are the ones the slide shows,
because they are what the week cards show too; the twilight is moved to
sit around them, so the slide never contradicts itself.

The sun's times are in the *plant's* timezone, not the browser's, and
they follow the server's clock, so they agree with the clock at the top
of the screen.

## When the sun does not rise

Inside the polar circles there are days when the sun does not rise at
all, and days when it does not set. The band is simply not drawn then —
there is nothing to draw — and the slide says nothing rather than
saying something wrong. The same happens if the place is not known.

The band is also left off on the smaller screens, where there is no
room for it; the details list is trimmed there anyway.

## Testing

- 371 tests (was 345): 12 for the sun's times against the forecast
  service's own figures, 13 for the twilight and the band.
- The layout check: all 48 viewports fit on one screen, no scrolling.
- The band was measured at 800×480, where the panel has 3 px to spare.
- The dark and light themes were both looked at.
