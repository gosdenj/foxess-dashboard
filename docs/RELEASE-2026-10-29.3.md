# Release 2026-10-29.3 — the phone fills its screen

The phone layout worked, and it left more than half the screen empty. On an
iPhone 13 the power slide used 376 pixels of an 811-pixel screen; on a large
phone it used 376 of 899. The rest was blank.

Three things go into it now: the **System panel**, the **state words** the
diagram used to say, and a **bigger battery ring** with **taller rows** in
today's figures.

## What was empty, and what is in it now

The phone's power slide, measured in Chrome:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the Live panel | 175 px | 218 px |
| the Today panel | 191 px | 223 px |
| the System panel | *dropped* | 343 px |
| the battery ring | 104 px | 120 px |
| today's rows | 25 px | 31 px |
| **the content in all** | **376 px** | **804 px** |

And what that leaves of the screen:

| A phone | empty before | empty after |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone 13, no browser chrome (390×844) | 435 px | **fits — 7 px to spare** |
| iPhone 13, with the URL bar (390×754) | 345 px | scrolls 83 px |
| iPhone SE (375×667) | 258 px | scrolls 170 px |
| Galaxy S20, with Chrome's bar (360×690) | 281 px | scrolls 147 px |
| Galaxy S20, no chrome (360×800) | 391 px | scrolls 37 px |
| small Android (360×640) | 231 px | scrolls 197 px |
| iPhone 13 Pro Max (430×932) | 523 px | 95 px to spare |

A phone that fits does not scroll, and a phone that has more in it than fits
scrolls to the end of it. Nothing is cut off either way.

## The System panel

It is the one panel the phone dropped and the Pi keeps, and it is what says
whether the machine is well: the firmware, the battery units, the minimum
state of charge, the signal, the API quota. The phone could show that the
battery was at 83% and nothing at all about the thing that holds the
battery.

Two rules hide it. One says "the layout is automatic" — because on the Pi
the rings diagram takes that space — and one says "the screen is small".
Neither is true of a phone, so the phone band asks for the panel back:

```css
body:where(:not(.no-system)):where(:not([data-layout="compact"])):where(:not([data-layout="tiny"]))
    .dash[data-slide="power"] .panel.system{display:flex}
```

The guards are written with `:where()`, which adds no specificity of its own.
That is the whole point of them: "no system", "compact" and "tiny" are
decisions the user has made, and they keep their own cases. A phone cannot
override a decision.

## The state words

The live tiles already carry a word — "generating", "consuming", "importing",
"discharging", "charging", "idle" — and the small-screen rules hide it. It
was hidden because the diagram said the same thing: on the Pi, the rings
carry the state of each part of the system.

A phone has no diagram. So the words go under the figures instead, and the
phone can say what the diagram used to say:

```
Solar          Grid            Batt
  5.19 kW     -1.02 kW        -3.00 kW
generating    exporting       charging
```

**Idle is the state most worth knowing about**, and idle is exactly the
state a bare number cannot express.

## The bigger ring, and the taller rows

The ring is the hero of the phone's power slide, so it goes from 104 px to
120 px — and no further. The ring sits *beside* the figures, so every pixel
of it is a pixel taken from them: at 150 px the widest value ("-12.34 kW")
no longer fits in a tile at 360 px wide, and a figure with its end cut off
is worse than a smaller ring.

Today's rows go from 25 px to 31 px, and the table's type from 12 px to
13 px, because a phone is read at arm's length and a table of six figures is
the thing being read.

## How it was checked

- **The layout sweep passes**: ten viewport sizes, three slides, two diagram
  styles — 60 cases, all of them.
- **`layout_check.py` learned three new things** about a phone:
  - the **System panel** has to be there — `NO-SYSTEM` otherwise;
  - the **state words** have to be there, and they have to have text in
    them — `NO-WORDS` otherwise;
  - **today's rows** have to be at least 28 px tall.
- **The geometry of every other display did not move.** The position, size
  and type size of every panel, tile, table row and SVG label, at nine sizes
  on three slides, measured before and after and compared:

  ```
  geometry identical: 21/27
    (the six that changed are the two phone sizes on the three slides)
  ```

  The only thing that changed in them is `touch-action`, from `none` to
  `pan-y` — which is the phone layout from the release before, not this one.
  Not one pixel of the Pi, the desktop, the laptop, the tablet, the small
  LCD, the ESP32 or the narrow panel moved.
- **Nothing is truncated** on any phone size, at any of the widest values
  the display ever shows (12.34 kW, 9999.99 kWh).
- **The slide does not clip its own content**, and everything below the fold
  is reachable by scrolling the stage — the same two measurements that
  caught the phone not scrolling at all.
