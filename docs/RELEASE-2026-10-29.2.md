# Release 2026-10-29.2 — the phone scrolls

The phone layout was meant to scroll. It did not. On a phone with more in
the slide than fits the screen, the bottom of it was cut off and could not
be reached, and the page sat still while the reader pushed on it.

## What was wrong

On a phone the slide is a **grid item** of the stage, and a grid hands its
items a definite height — the stage's own height. So the slide was exactly
as tall as the screen, and the panels below the fold were **clipped** by
the slide's own `overflow:hidden`.

Clipped is not the same as scrollable. The stage had `overflow-y:auto` and
nothing to scroll, because the content had been cut off before it ever
reached the stage.

The usage slide is the one that shows it: the chart, the Octopus figures and
today's figures together are taller than a phone's screen. The power slide
was short enough to fit, which is why it looked fine and the problem was
not obvious.

## The fix

One line. The stage is a plain block on a phone, rather than a grid:

```css
.stage{display:block}
```

The slide is not a grid item any more, so it grows with its content, and
the stage scrolls it. The bottom of the slide is now where the reader can
get to it.

## How much it was clipping

Measured in Chrome with a real touch drag, on the slides as they are:

| A phone | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone 13, no browser chrome (390×844) | fits | fits |
| iPhone 13, with the URL bar (390×754) | **65 px clipped** | scrolls |
| iPhone SE (375×667) | **152 px clipped** | scrolls |
| Galaxy S20, with Chrome's bar (360×690) | **129 px clipped** | scrolls |
| Galaxy S20, no chrome (360×800) | **19 px clipped** | scrolls |
| small Android (360×640) | **179 px clipped** | scrolls |

A phone's visible viewport is much shorter than its screen — the browser's
own bars take 90 to 110 pixels of it — which is why the usage slide
overflows on nearly every phone even though it fits the bare screen size.

Where the slide fits, there is nothing to scroll, and nothing is missing.
That is the honest behaviour: a phone is allowed to scroll, not required
to.

## What was checked

- The slide no longer clips its own content on any phone size: the
  measurement is `dashClipped = 0` for every slide at every size, and the
  bottom of the last panel is reachable by scrolling the stage.
- A **real touch drag** scrolls the stage — 19, 28, 152 and 179 pixels of
  real finger movement on the sizes that need it.
- The **sideways swipe still turns the deck**, on a phone and on the Pi.
  The gesture code was not touched: `touch-action:pan-y` gives the browser
  the vertical drag and leaves the horizontal one.
- The slide transition still works, and the leaving slide's copy is the
  same colour as the slide arriving over it, so there is no flash.
- The geometry of every existing display was measured again and compared
  with the state before the phone layout: **21 of 27 identical**, and the
  six that changed are the two phone sizes on the three slides. The Pi, the
  desktop, the laptop, the tablet, the small LCD, the ESP32 and the narrow
  panel did not move.

## The check that would have caught it

`layout_check.py` now measures two things it did not measure before:

- `dashClipped` — the slide must not be shorter than its own content.
- `dashReach` — everything in the slide has to be reachable by scrolling
  the stage.

Against the old layout it reports `SLIDE-CLIPPED:19` on the usage slide at
360×800, and fails. It passes now.

`tests/test_phone_layout.py` gained three tests for the rule itself: the
stage is a block on a phone, it is not left as a grid, and a slide with
little in it still fills the screen.

## Testing

- 488 tests (was 485): three for the scroll rule. The only failure is the
  pre-existing one — `test_esp32_render` needs Pillow, which is not
  installed.
- The full layout sweep passes: ten viewport sizes, three slides, two
  diagram styles.
- The swipe was verified with a real touch drag on a phone viewport and on
  the Pi's: both still turn the deck.
