# Release 2026-10-27.3 — yesterday, for comparison

The **Today** panel on the Usage slide carries yesterday's figure under
each of today's, so that the two can be read against each other.

## What it looks like

```
SOLAR          HOUSE         BATTERY        GRID
10.15 kWh      4.29 kWh      0.53 kWh       8.81 kWh
yesterday      yesterday     yesterday      yesterday
12.38 kWh      3.92 kWh      0.72 kWh       6.52 kWh
```

The same six tiles as before — solar, house, battery, grid, the share
of the solar that stayed on site, and the cost — each with yesterday's
figure under it, in a smaller and quieter hand. The word and the number
are on separate lines, so the number can carry its unit: kilowatt-hours
for the energy, a percentage for the share, and pounds for the cost.

There are no arrows and no colours: the two numbers are there to be
read against each other, and nothing more is claimed about them.

## Where yesterday comes from

The energy figures come out of the inverter's own register. The monthly
report gives one value per day of the month, and it has already been
fetched for today's figures — so yesterday's is simply read one day
further back, and costs nothing extra. On the first of the month
yesterday was in the month before, so that month's register is fetched
once and then kept, rather than every five minutes for the rest of the
day.

The cost comes from the Octopus Mini, which keeps a day or two of its
own half-hourly readings. Yesterday's is only reported when the whole
of it is still there: a part of a day is worse than none at all, and
would be compared with today as though it were the whole thing.

The inverter's register is used rather than the dashboard's own
history, because the history only goes back as far as the dashboard
has been running, and yesterday's figures should be there on the first
morning after a restart.

## What it is not

Yesterday is a **whole day** and today is not. So until the evening,
today's figure will usually be the smaller of the two — not because
the day is going badly, but because the day is not over yet. The
figures say where the day has got to, against the whole of the day
before it.

Where there is no yesterday to compare with — the meter has forgotten
it, or the inverter has not reported it — the line is left out rather
than filled in with something that is not there.

## The tiles

The tiles were sparse, and the comparison fills the space under the
figures. The content is now centred in the tile, which is what the
stylesheet had been asking for all along — it set `justify-content`
without setting the flex that would make it mean anything.

  dark and the light theme: it is not clipped anywhere.
